Original main question
For A contained in [1,n], every two distinct subsets must have unequal products.
The requested upper bound is pi(n) + pi(sqrt(n)) + o(sqrt(n)/log(n)).
The source has no restriction to subsets of equal cardinality, disjoint subsets,
nonempty subsets, or squarefree elements. All these distinctions matter.

The interval is exactly Finset.Icc 1 N. subsetProduct is the ordinary finite
product, so the empty product is 1. DistinctSubsetProducts is injectivity on the
entire powerset. In particular, an admissible set cannot contain 1, since its
singleton and the empty subset would have equal products. This exclusion is a
consequence, not an extra hypothesis. Zero is excluded by the interval.
The finite maximum g is Finset.sup over all admissible subsets. Empty-set
admissibility and an attained maximum are proved. At n=0 the interval is empty
and g=0; no unexplained nonempty-maximum convention changes this value.
Nat.primeCounting (Nat.sqrt n) counts precisely the primes at most real sqrt(n).

Shared predicate and extremal-function bridge
DistinctSubsetProducts.lean defines products for arbitrary commutative monoids,
including naturals, and generates the additive twins by to_additive. The finite
predicate quantifies over all subsets and is equivalent to product injectivity
on the powerset. Its deciding instance does not alter its proposition.
Statement795.HasDistinctSubsetProducts abbreviates this finite predicate.
Bridge795.distinctSubsetProducts_iff establishes equivalence with upstream.
Statement795.g uses sSup of the possible cardinalities, matching PR #6338's
presentation. Bridge795.g_eq proves equality with the upstream finite maximum:
the supremum set is nonempty (empty subset, cardinality zero), bounded by n,
and both inequalities are obtained from exactly the same admissible subsets.
The argument covers every n, including zero. There is no replacement of the
extremal function with an unproved approximation or an assumed maximizer.

Exact final target
Statement795.Target asks for a real-valued error e, little-o of sqrt(n)/log(n)
at infinity, with the upper inequality for every natural n. This is the standard
error-function formulation of the question. The bridge chooses the signed
excess g(n)-pi(n)-pi(Nat.sqrt n). The upstream theorem proves this very function
is little-o. Substituting g_eq makes the required inequality an equality for
every n. Values of log at zero or one cause no asymptotic issue, and no finite
exceptions are silently discarded. The outer True iff Target corresponds to
the solved affirmative answer convention. No additional mathematical
hypotheses appear in this endpoint.

The upstream theorem Erdos795.erdos_795 independently presents the equivalent
positive-epsilon/eventually upper bound. The file also proves the primes and
prime squares construction and baseline pi(n)+pi(Nat.sqrt n) <= g(n), for all n.
Its factorization argument distinguishes the exponent choices 0,1,2,3 for each
prime, correctly allowing both p and p^2 in the construction.

Limits of the claim
Raghavan v2 Theorem 1.3 gives the stronger error O(n^(5/12)), without an o(1)
in the exponent. The website quotes an older/weaker error presentation.
This verification establishes the original main little-o question and the
prime/prime-square baseline. It does not establish the sharper 5/12 error,
the pi(n^(1/3))/3-O(1) improvement, the resulting disproof of the stronger
Erdős series speculation, or the paper's squarefree variants. No claim that
the entire paper is formalized follows from these endpoints.
